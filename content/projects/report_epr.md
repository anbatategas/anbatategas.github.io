This report investigates the phenomenon of Electron Paramagnetic Resonance (EPR) by observing the resonant behavior of the unpaired electron in the DPPH organic radical. Conducted as part of the "Condensed Matter Physics" laboratory coursework at NTUA, the experiment bridges classical electrodynamics with quantum mechanical models of electron spin and magnetic susceptibility.

The physical phenomena and experimental methodologies explored in this report cover:
*   **Theoretical Framework:** Comprehensive analysis of EPR using both classical Larmor precession models ($\Omega_L = \gamma_e B_0$) and the quantum mechanical formulation of Zeeman energy splitting ($\Delta E = g_S \mu_B B_0$) for spin-1/2 particles.
*   **Signal Detection & Bloch Equations:** Utilization of an AC bridge circuit, a 146 MHz RF generator, and Helmholtz coils with 50 Hz modulation to capture the EPR signal on an oscilloscope. The resonance condition was identified via the global maximum of the imaginary part of the complex magnetic susceptibility ($\chi''$), strictly adhering to Bloch's equations.
*   **Electron Spin g-factor Determination:** Experimental calculation of the Landé g-factor for the electron spin ($g_S$) by determining the resonance static magnetic field ($B_0$). The resulting value ($g_S = 2.00 \pm 0.07$) strongly aligns with the theoretical free-electron constant ($2.00232$).
*   **Spin-Spin Relaxation Time:** Analysis of the local magnetic field interactions between neighboring dipoles. By measuring the resonance curve's half-maximum broadening ($\Delta B_{0.5}$), the transverse spin-spin relaxation time was experimentally calculated ($T_2 = (2.4 \pm 0.6) \cdot 10^{-8}$ $\mathrm{s}$).
