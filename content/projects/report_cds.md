This laboratory report investigates the photoconductive properties of Cadmium Sulfide ($\mathrm{CdS}$) semiconductors, analyzing how electrical conductivity responds to variations in incident light intensity and photon frequency. Conducted as part of the "Condensed Matter Physics" laboratory coursework at NTUA, the experiment connects semiconductor band theory with macroscopic optical responses.

The physical phenomena and experimental methodologies explored in this report cover:
*   **Fundamental Absorption & Carrier Generation:** Theoretical overview of photon-induced electron transitions across the direct bandgap of $\mathrm{CdS}$. Analysis of carrier generation, recombination dynamics, and the resulting equilibrium photoconductivity.
*   **Intensity Dependence:** Measurement of photocurrent-voltage ($I_{ph}-V$) characteristics using a variable polarization setup (analyzer-polarizer) to modulate the intensity of incident white light. The results empirically verified Malus's Law and confirmed the linear relationship between steady-state photoconductivity and incident light intensity ($\sigma_{ph} \propto I$).
*   **Spectral Response:** Prism-based spectral decomposition of the light source to measure the photocurrent as a function of incident photon energy (wavelength). Raw data was normalized against the theoretical Planck blackbody radiation spectrum to extract the pure spectral response curve of the semiconductor.
*   **Bandgap Estimation:** Identification of the absorption edge from the spectral response curve, leading to an experimental approximation of the $\mathrm{CdS}$ bandgap energy ($E_g \approx 2.1 \pm 0.3$ $\mathrm{eV}$) at room temperature. The report discusses the distinct response regions resulting from surface recombination and bulk absorption effects.
