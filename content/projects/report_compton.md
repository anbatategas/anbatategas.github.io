This report details the experimental investigation of Compton scattering and the interaction of gamma rays with matter. Conducted as part of the "Nuclear Physics & Elementary Particles Laboratory" coursework at NTUA, the experiment leverages scintillation detectors to construct and analyze energy spectra from various radioactive sources.

The physical phenomena and experimental methodologies explored in this report cover:
*   **Gamma Ray Interactions:** Theoretical and experimental analysis of the three primary interaction mechanisms within a NaI(Tl) scintillator: the Photoelectric effect, Compton scattering, and Pair production.
*   **Spectrum Construction:** Operation of a Photomultiplier Tube (PMT) and a Multi-Channel Analyzer (MCA) to record and shape pulse-height diagrams. Analysis of the resulting spectra to identify characteristic features, including the photopeak, Compton edge, and backscatter peak.
*   **Electron Rest Mass Determination:** Utilizing the measured energies of the photopeak, Compton edge, and backscatter peak from $\mathrm{{}^{22}Na}$, $\mathrm{{}^{60}Co}$, and $\mathrm{{}^{137}Cs}$ sources to experimentally calculate the electron rest mass ($m_e c^2$).
*   **Relativistic Kinematics:** Calculation of the maximum kinetic and total relativistic energy of the scattered electrons. The results are compared against classical mechanics predictions, clearly demonstrating the relativistic mass-energy equivalence at speeds approaching the speed of light ($v_{max} \approx 0.92c$).
