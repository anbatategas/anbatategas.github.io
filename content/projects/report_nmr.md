This report investigates the phenomenon of Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) by measuring the magnetic susceptibility and resonant behavior of hydrogen protons in glycerin. Conducted as part of the "Atomic & Molecular Physics Laboratory" coursework at NTUA, the experiment bridged classical electrodynamics with quantum mechanical models of nuclear spin.

The physical phenomena and experimental methodologies explored in this report cover:
*   **Theoretical Framework:** Comprehensive analysis of NMR using both classical Larmor precession models and quantum mechanical Zeeman energy splitting ($E = \pm \frac{1}{2}g_N \beta_N B_0$) for spin-1/2 particles.
*   **Signal Detection & Bloch Equations:** Utilization of an AC bridge circuit and an oscilloscope to capture the NMR signal. The resonance condition was identified via the zero-crossing of the real part of the complex magnetic susceptibility ($\chi'$), adhering strictly to Bloch's equations.
*   **Magnetic Resonance Saturation:** Qualitative observation and theoretical explanation of the saturation effect. It was demonstrated that as the amplitude of the transverse RF magnetic field ($B_x$) increases, the population difference between energy states diminishes, causing a corresponding drop in the NMR signal intensity.
*   **Nuclear Landé g-factor Determination:** Experimental calculation of the proton Landé g-factor ($g_N$) by mapping the linear relationship between the applied static magnetic field ($B_0$) and the resonant RF frequency ($f_0$). The resulting value ($g_N = 5.61 \pm 0.15$) aligned closely with the theoretical constant ($5.5857$).
