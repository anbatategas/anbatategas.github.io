This report examines the linear emission spectrum of the Sodium ($\mathrm{Na}$) atom, focusing on the fine structure splitting of its characteristic yellow D-lines. Conducted as part of the "Atomic & Molecular Physics Laboratory" coursework at NTUA, the experiment utilizes optical spectroscopy to measure atomic emission wavelengths and quantify quantum mechanical spin-orbit interactions.

The theoretical analysis and optical measurements developed in this study cover:
*   **Optical Spectroscopy:** Use of a reflection grating spectrometer equipped with a high-density optical grating ($N = 1800$ lines/mm) to resolve the visible emission spectrum of a Sodium vapor lamp.
*   **Wavelength Determination:** Experimental measurement of the diffraction angles to calculate the wavelengths of the red, yellow-green, and yellow spectral lines.
*   **Spin-Orbit Coupling (L-S Coupling):** Theoretical modeling of the Sodium D-line doublet ($\mathrm{D_1}$ and $\mathrm{D_2}$), caused by the internal magnetic interaction between the valence electron's spin and its orbital angular momentum during $3^2P_{1/2} \rightarrow 3^2S_{1/2}$ and $3^2P_{3/2} \rightarrow 3^2S_{1/2}$ transitions.
*   **Energy Splitting Calculation:** Empirical calculation of the fine structure energy splitting ($\Delta E_{D_1,D_2}$) derived directly from the resolved doublet wavelengths. The report also explores the theoretical limits and resolving boundaries of the reflection grating for higher-order diffraction fringes.
