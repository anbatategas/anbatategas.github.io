This report investigates the quantization of atomic energy levels through the Franck-Hertz experiment, utilizing a Neon (Ne) gas tube. Conducted as part of the "Atomic & Molecular Physics Laboratory" coursework at NTUA, the experiment provides direct empirical evidence for the Bohr model by tracking the energy losses of free electrons undergoing inelastic collisions with Neon atoms.

The physical phenomena and experimental methodologies explored in this report cover:
*   **Thermionic Emission & Contact Potential:** Production of free electrons via a heated $\mathrm{LaB_6}$ cathode. The external contact potential difference between the $\mathrm{LaB_6}$ cathode and the Nickel ($\mathrm{Ni}$) anode was experimentally determined through current-voltage curve shifts ($U_{\epsilon\pi} = 1.6 \pm 0.5$ V).
*   **Inelastic Collisions & Energy Quantization:** Measurement of the electron current ($I_3$) reaching the collection plate as a function of the accelerating voltage ($U_2$). The resulting periodic current drops successfully confirmed the hypothesis that electrons lose their entire kinetic energy upon inelastic collisions.
*   **First Excitation Energy:** Experimental determination of Neon's first excitation energy ($E_1 = 17.7 \pm 1.3$ eV) by mapping the voltage intervals between consecutive current minima.
*   **Visible Emission Spectra:** Observation of discrete red luminous fringes within the primary acceleration chamber, corresponding to the $3p \rightarrow 3s$ (or $E_2 \rightarrow E_1$) atomic de-excitations.
